# Chapter 6: Living With Your Automations

A recipe that works on the day you set it up is the easy part. The
harder part comes three months later, when a folder has moved, a
password has changed, a colleague wants a copy and nobody remembers
why a task runs at half past six. This chapter is about those months.

Mia has run her easy recipes since October. Jonas has the medium ones
for his team's documents and mail, and Lena keeps a dozen jobs running
under the job server of Chapter 5. Each of them meets the questions of
this chapter at a different scale, and the answers are the same for
all three:

1. **Look at your automations once a month.** A short check finds most
   problems before they cost you anything.
2. **Change a program the safe way.** Copy it, write the test, make the
   change, run it dry. The section on changing a program walks through
   a real change to the setup wizard.
3. **Share without becoming the help desk.** A recipe you hand out
   needs a few things it does not need on your own computer.
4. **Talk to IT before they talk to you.** Most IT departments are
   glad to answer a clear question, and the recipes of this book give
   you clear answers to theirs.
5. **Retire what no longer pays.** An automation you do not need is a
   small risk with no return.
6. **Read your ledger.** After three months the ledger of Chapter 1
   tells you which recipes give back time, and where that time went.

The chapter ends with the question the whole book is about: what to do
with the hours.
