# How to Read This Book

<!-- toc -->

<!-- pagebreak -->

This book is a toolbox. You do not have to read it from the first page to
the last. Chapter 1 asks where your week goes and which chores are worth
handing to a program. Chapter 2 installs jdBasic and runs the setup
wizard. After that, every recipe stands on its own: pick the chore that
annoys you most and start there.

The recipes come in three levels. The fifteen **easy** recipes in Chapter 3
run as soon as the wizard has switched them on; you change their settings
in one file and never have to touch the code. The **medium** recipes in
Chapter 4 expect you to read a program of a hundred lines and change a few
of them. The **expert** recipes in Chapter 5 are small systems that run on
their own, with logs, retries and tests.

Every program in this book is printed from the file that was tested. Each
one runs in the jdBasic interpreter and also as a compiled program, and
each comes with a test that proves it does what the text says. You find
all of them in the folder `recipes`, next to the manuscript of this book.

Every recipe ends with a box like this one:

> **Balance dividend**
> The minutes the recipe gives back to you in a normal week. Write them
> into your ledger (Chapter 1) and decide what to do with them before the
> week fills them for you.
