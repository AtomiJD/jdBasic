# The King and the Kingdom

The kingdom is ruled by King Aldred III of the House of Marrenwald.
He has worn the iron crown for nineteen years. Most people in the
hills have never seen him in person - the king sits in the capital
at Caerstoke, three weeks' ride to the south. The royal banner is a
silver heron on a green field.

If anyone in the hills asks "who is the king" or "what is the king's
name", the answer is the same: King Aldred III. He is not loved
here, but he is not hated either. He is mostly absent.

# The War Against the Marchfolk

Three years ago, King Aldred III sent his army into the eastern
hills to push back the Marchfolk - a loose confederation of clans
from across the eastern range. The campaign was supposed to last one
summer. It lasted three years. It ended without a treaty.

The Marchfolk were never really defeated; they simply stopped
pushing. King Aldred III simply stopped pushing back. The official
chronicles call this "a strategic consolidation". Soldiers who lived
through it use harder language.

# The Withdrawal

Two winters ago, King Aldred III pulled his army out of the eastern
hills. The garrisons emptied within a fortnight. The watchtowers
were left standing but unmanned. The royal levy has not returned to
these hills in eighteen months.

Sir Gareth was among the knights stationed in the eastern hills when
the order to withdraw came. He was given the choice to follow his
unit back to Caerstoke or to stay. He stayed. So did a handful of
others, mostly older men with no families left to return to.

# Why the Withdrawal Happened

The withdrawal was triggered by a failed assault on the old keep on
the northern peak. King Aldred III sent a full column up the peak
in late autumn three years back. The column did not return. The
royal chronicles record it as "lost to weather".

Survivors who limped back the following spring told a different
story to anyone who would listen. Something was already in the keep.
Not Marchfolk. Not bandits. King Aldred III's chronicles do not
record what the survivors said. Sir Gareth was on the recovery
patrol that found them, and he has not spoken of it since.

After that, King Aldred III's court lost the appetite for the war.
Within a year the army was being pulled back. Within two it was
fully gone.

# The River Village Now

The river village three days west still flies King Aldred III's
banner - the silver heron on green - out of habit. No royal levy has
come in eighteen months. The mayor is the blacksmith Hilda, by
default rather than by election.

The Marchfolk have not pushed south. Whatever appetite they had for
the hills seems to have ended with the last campaign. Older folk in
the village say the Marchfolk were never the real threat to begin
with.

# What People Believe About the King Now

Common knowledge about King Aldred III, as everyone in the hills
would say it:

- The royal levy will return when there is gold to pay it. Not
  before.
- The Marchfolk are spent. So is King Aldred III's army.
- The keep on the northern peak is to be left alone. The few who
  suggest otherwise are generally older or drunk.
- Magic, if it ever was a force in the war, played no public role.
- The southern academy of magic was closed two winters ago by King
  Aldred III's direct order, "pending review". The review has not
  happened.

# Frequently Asked

"Who is the king?" - King Aldred III of Marrenwald.
"How long has he ruled?" - Nineteen winters.
"Where does he live?" - In the capital at Caerstoke, three weeks
  south.
"Is he a good king?" - Depends who you ask. Sir Gareth would say
  "he was, once". Vex would say "he keeps to his end of the realm
  and we keep to ours".
"What is his banner?" - A silver heron on a green field.
